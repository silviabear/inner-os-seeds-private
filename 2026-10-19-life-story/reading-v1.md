## What is literally there

The raw text is a short first-person excerpt (it opens mid-sentence —
"...and people welcomed me" — so earlier context is missing from this raw
file). It describes two periods that contrast directly: an earlier one
where an approach to others was welcomed despite "insensitivity," and high
school, where the same approach was "interpreted as absolute weirdness,"
leading to being "severely bullied." The piece is explicit that the harm it
dwells on is not the visible abuse but "what's unsaid" — a long period of
not being able to name a felt state ("I was clueless for a very long time,
something was off... but I couldn't name it"). The naming arrives
secondhand and almost by accident: a cousin, in a different 600-person
class, reports that an acquaintance called the writer "this super weird and
notorious person in the class" without knowing it was about her — which is
the moment of realizing "I was shun[ned]." The text adds a second,
confusing accusation (being seen as showing off sexiness to seduce guys)
that it says it had "no clue of," and closes on an unresolved note — "up
until today, there were still part of it that looked like a mystery" — and
a reframe offered by a partner: that the bullying "might actually come out
of something special in me."

Verbs/images that recur: welcomed vs. bullied; clueless; couldn't name it;
told offhandedly; realized; shun; no clue; mystery; something special.
